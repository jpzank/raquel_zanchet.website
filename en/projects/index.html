Jardim da Amazônia Lodge - Mato Grosso

Nature Lodge / 1997 - present

Context

Three hundred kilometres north of Cuiabá, in the southern Amazon, inside a significant ecological corridor: a large fragment of forest in the transition zone between the Cerrado savanna and the Amazon. The trees there are markedly lower, which makes canopy species easier to observe, along with a wide range of Amazonian specialities.

Role

Conception and implementation of the lodge, from 1997.

The work

Wildlife watching is the core product, and it depends directly on the forest being left standing. More than 530 bird species have been recorded on the property, several of them emblematic, such as the harpy eagle, considered the most powerful eagle on the planet. The cone-billed tanager (Conothraupis mesoleuca), listed as critically endangered by the IUCN, was found in the Alto Juruena basin in 2006, and this appears to be the largest known population of the species.

Outcome

In operation since 1997, with its own registered eBird hotspot. In 2022 the lodge began supporting the Jardim da Amazônia Research Station.

Development: Jardim da Amazônia Field Station / 2022

Raquel Zanchet is involved in creating the Jardim da Amazônia Research Station, an initiative led by Instituto Ecótono in partnership with Brazilian and international institutions including the Applied Ecology Group of Sinop (GECAS, UFMT), UNEMAT, the University of Salford, the Brazilian Primatology Society and ICMBio. Based in São José do Rio Claro, Mato Grosso, with support from Jardim da Amazônia Lodge, the station was conceived in 2022 to study and protect local fauna, particularly birds and medium to large mammals, including threatened primates.

Work began in 2023, focused on monitoring species such as the Mittermeier's saki (Pithecia mittermeieri), the Peruvian spider monkey (Ateles chamek) and rare birds, to understand their behaviour and habitat. The research also aims to promote sustainable tourism by encouraging wildlife watching. Beyond protecting biodiversity, keeping the forest standing is essential to the springs that feed the Arinos river, which makes conservation an economic opportunity for landowners in the region.

Zank Hotel Boutique - Salvador, Bahia

Retrofit and boutique hotel / 2007 - 2015

Context

An early twentieth-century townhouse in Rio Vermelho, Salvador, to be turned into a boutique hotel without losing its historic architecture.

Role

Co-founder of the property.

The work

Retrofit of the townhouse into a twenty-key hotel, with minimalist architecture by Marlem Vilela and interior design by Judith Pottecher. Few keys, so that personal service holds up, with refined decoration and a strong Brazilian identity. Distinctive services, such as breakfast cooked to order, make up an intimate experience aimed at demanding guests.

Outcome

Opened in 2007. Featured in the New York Times in 2010 and winner of the Interior Design Award in the Hotels category from Casa Claudia magazine in 2011.

The proposed project tries to improve the relationship between the buildings and the city; the strong presence of nature and the way it sits in the urban landscape were essential in redefining a relationship with the surroundings.

The building speaks to its context cleanly and clearly, in a rich dialogue when it comes to the inside and outside as an extension of the natural landscape.

Architect: Marlem Vilela

3 Ecosystems: Pantanal, Amazon and Cerrado

Took part in setting up the route, 1999

The "Mato Grosso, 3 Ecosystems" route was set up in collaboration with André von Thurnoy, of Pantanal Explorer. A pioneering programme connecting the state's three main biomes: the floodplains of the Pantanal, the Cerrado savanna and the Amazon rainforest.

Casa de Campo - Nobres, Mato Grosso

Project in partnership with Phillipe Sancho

Natural building with sustainable techniques and local materials. The proposal added rooms with private verandas, using rammed earth as the main element to keep the character of the countryside and provide natural thermal comfort.
